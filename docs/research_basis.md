# Journal research basis for the near-miss prototype

This project is a **small, stationary, single-target lab adaptation**. None of these papers validates the team's exact HC-SR04 + LPC1768 setup, and the present ML scores are synthetic. The research question is whether a small laptop-trained 0.5-second ultrasonic predictor could improve held-out warning performance compared with a constant-speed rule. The team's servo sweep stays unchanged; the firmware now exports ultrasonic data over UART0. PIR is not used in the ML experiment. This is a proposed extension, not a claim of worldwide novelty or a claim that ML is already running on the board.

All 15 entries below are journal articles, not conference papers. The links resolve to publisher DOI/article pages. The papers are ordered by connection to warning decisions, sensor measurements and evaluation.

| # | Journal paper | What we take from it; what we **do not** copy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | [Jiao et al., *A unified probabilistic approach to traffic conflict detection*, Analytic Methods in Accident Research 45 (2025) 100369](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amar.2024.100369) | Compare context-aware prediction with simple risk rules; we do not reproduce their Bayesian traffic model. |
| 2 | [Zhao et al., *A Spatial-State-Based Omni-Directional Collision Warning System for Intelligent Vehicles*, IEEE T-ITS 25(10) (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1109/TITS.2024.3387942) | Define an explicit protected zone and warning state; our one-angle sensor is not omni-directional. |
| 3 | [Han et al., *Adaptive ROI for Collision Warning Mitigation Based on Road Geometry and Kinematics*, IEEE Sensors Journal 25(3) (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1109/JSEN.2024.3512947) | Reduce irrelevant warnings by testing a marked area, without claiming full road geometry. |
| 4 | [Shao et al., *Adaptive forward collision warning system for hazmat truck drivers*, Accident Analysis & Prevention 191 (2023) 107221](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aap.2023.107221) | Treat warning timing as a variable to measure; our prototype does not model truck drivers. |
| 5 | [Bao and Wang, *Optimization of Forward Collision Warning Algorithm Considering Truck Driver Response Behavior Characteristics*, Accident Analysis & Prevention 198 (2024) 107450](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aap.2023.107450) | Compare an adaptive warning to a fixed-rule baseline, without copying their driver model. |
| 6 | [Xie et al., *Personalized forward collision warning model with learning from human preferences*, Accident Analysis & Prevention 208 (2024) 107791](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aap.2024.107791) | Evaluate nuisance warnings as well as correct warnings; we are not training from human preferences. |
| 7 | [Fu et al., *How predictive-forward-collision-warning reduces the collision risk of leading vehicle driver*, Accident Analysis & Prevention 211 (2025) 107891](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aap.2024.107891) | Ask whether prediction produces useful warning lead time; our bench result is not a real vehicle safety outcome. |
| 8 | [Olufowobi et al., *Safety effectiveness of forward collision warning systems in the vehicle fleet*, Accident Analysis & Prevention 218 (2025) 108078](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aap.2025.108078) | Evaluate warnings by events and scenarios, not only distance error; our sample is a controlled lab trial. |
| 9 | [Ahsan et al., *Vehicle-Pedestrian near miss analysis at signalized mid-block crossings*, Journal of Safety Research 91 (2024) 68–84](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsr.2024.08.006) | Use a clear definition of a near miss; our 30 cm zone is a bench proxy, not their traffic measure. |
| 10 | [*Hybrid pedestrian positioning system using wearable inertial sensors and ultrasonic ranging*, Defence Technology 33 (2024) 327–338](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dt.2023.11.006) | Shows the motivation for future multi-sensor work; our current prediction uses ultrasonic distance only. |
| 11 | [Xu and Bowers, *Hazard warning modalities and timing thresholds for older drivers with impaired vision*, Accident Analysis & Prevention 202 (2024) 107599](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aap.2024.107599) | Record when an alert occurs; human-driver response is outside our experiment. |
| 12 | [Yang et al., *Drivers' reactions to real-world forward collision warnings*, Accident Analysis & Prevention 210 (2025) 107853](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aap.2024.107853) | Inspect time sequences around warning events; our tests do not measure driving behavior. |
| 13 | [Westhofen et al., *Criticality Metrics for Automated Driving: A Review and Suitability Analysis*, Archives of Computational Methods in Engineering 30 (2023) 1–35](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11831-022-09788-7) | Choose metrics that match the actual task: future distance, lead time, false alerts and misses. This is a **review**, not an experiment on our hardware. |
| 14 | [Cárdenas et al., *Experimental Evaluation of a Head-On Collision Warning System Fusing Machine Learning and Decentralized Radio Sensing*, IEEE Sensors Journal 24(13) (2024) 21520–21532](https://doi.org/10.1109/JSEN.2024.3403492) | Compare learned and simpler warning methods experimentally; we do not have their radio sensing setup. |
| 15 | [Izquierdo et al., *Pedestrian detection using a MEMS acoustic array mounted on a moving vehicle*, Sensors and Actuators A: Physical 376 (2024) 115586](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sna.2024.115586) | Recognize that sensing a pedestrian is harder than measuring a single range; our HC-SR04 cannot identify a person. |

Describe the current work as an **existing board scan with added serial logging and an offline predictive-warning experiment**. Scanning measurements at different angles cannot be used as a time series for one target. The simulated data only verify the software workflow. The 1,000 real readings and fixed-view hardware evaluation still need to be collected in the lab.
